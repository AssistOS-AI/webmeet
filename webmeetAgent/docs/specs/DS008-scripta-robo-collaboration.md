# DS008 - SCRIPTA Robo Collaboration

## Introduction

WebMeet integrates SCRIPTA through one native `scripta-document` blackboard widget. Workspace Markdown state is authoritative; room state contains attachment metadata, the active resource, and the shared document/paragraph view focus.

## Core Content

Creating a room idempotently creates `/WebMeet/<room-slug>-<short-room-id>/`. Renaming the room does not move this folder. Room creation does not create an implicit document. A participant may create a document in the room folder, while an authenticated workspace user may also open a workspace Markdown path. A canonical path may be attached to only one room at a time.

Vision, Plan, and General are creation templates, not document roles. Vision generation produces one chapter and at least three distinct aspect paragraphs. Plan generation produces the requested chapter structure with at least one paragraph per chapter. Their creation form requires a work objective because it supplies the generated initial content. General creates one chapter with one empty paragraph without invoking AI, so its form does not show or submit a work objective. After creation, every result is the same SCRIPTA Markdown format. Opening ordinary Markdown preserves its title, chapter titles, paragraph content, and structure while adding stable SCRIPTA identifiers and an initial variant idempotently.

The blackboard applies CRAP visual hierarchy: creation, collaboration, history, and appearance controls are repeated as aligned toolbar groups without visible text labels, card backgrounds, or group borders. Proximity and fine vertical separators distinguish the groups, appearance stays on the trailing edge, the canvas has stronger surface contrast and a restrained grid, and an empty board presents one concise starting instruction.

The blackboard contains at most one SCRIPTA widget: `robo_scripta_document` with type `scripta-document`, and no SCRIPTA widget exists when no document is attached. Deleting the document removes its file, room attachment, and widget projection. The SCRIPTA widget has a 600 by 400 pixel minimum footprint and persists user resize geometry; Poll and Bullets use the same resize handles with minimums of 260 by 132 and 320 by 190 respectively. The blackboard remains sized to its available surface and provides horizontal and vertical scrolling when the viewport is below those minimums or positioned widgets extend beyond the visible area. CSS-only invisible overflow spacers reserve 10 pixels below widgets and 40 pixels on the right for contextual widget actions; top and left receive 10 pixel margins. SCRIPTA keeps its outer widget overflow visible while its document body remains the internal scroll container, so edge widgets and their move/delete controls remain reachable without JavaScript scroll-extent calculations. Wheel input over the SCRIPTA widget scrolls the blackboard whenever it can move in that direction; at a blackboard boundary, the document retains its own content scrolling. Its document view renders every chapter and active paragraph variant. SCRIPTA command/modal integration and SCRIPTA rendering are separate panel modules. Repeated SCRIPTA markup is declared as inert WebSkel component templates; the renderer clones those templates and assigns document text through `textContent`. Chapter, paragraph, and navigation clicks use `data-local-action` and one presenter dispatcher, while keyboard and semantic `scripta-variants-view` events retain their purpose-specific listeners. The document header contains the document name and a compact, visibly button-styled add-chapter control rendered as the short `Chapter` label followed by a plus icon. A new chapter is stored as `Chapter N`; clicking its title opens a dedicated inline editor with explicit Save and Cancel controls, without a separate rename action or blur-based persistence. Contextual chapter actions are compact icons on the right for adding a paragraph, moving the chapter exactly one position up/down, and deleting it. Paragraphs do not have names, and their document-view cards contain only active content or a non-persistent empty placeholder; clicking a paragraph opens its full variant editing view, so no separate paragraph edit action is rendered. Contextual paragraph actions are compact icons on the right: up/down move exactly one position within the chapter and delete removes the paragraph. Its paragraph view uses Explorer's whitelisted `scripta-variants-view` component, shared with Advanced Editor, to render variant tabs, owner-only inline editing and deletion, deterministic active-variant selection, like/dislike controls, manual variants, and AI reformulation. Every admitted participant may vote on every variant, but only the participant who added a variant may edit or delete it; deleting the last variant is rejected. The canonical paragraph-variant operations are `p-variant-add`, `p-variant-edit`, `p-variant-delete`, `p-variant-vote`, `p-variant-vote-withdraw`, and `p-variant-reformulate`, exposed as `scripta-p-variant-*` blackboard events. WebMeet maps the component's semantic events to RoboTeam events and does not let the shared component persist state directly. Paragraph mode has a compact navigation toolbar where Document repeats the same button treatment as the `Paragraph`-labelled Prev/Next controls; icon and position distinguish the return action. Arrow icons are CSS-rendered and unavailable directions are disabled at document boundaries. Context information is visually subordinate to editable prose: the chapter title is secondary context, while generated Chapter/Paragraph ordinals use small muted monospace text without a background, border, or action-like shape. Previous/next navigation crosses chapter boundaries without wrapping. Returning to document view keeps the paragraph focus so each browser can scroll the corresponding anchor into view.

Variant selection is shared room state through `scripta-p-variant-select`, so every participant sees the same selected card. Starting and cancelling an owner-authorized edit are canonical RoboTeam presentation events. While the owner types, a debounced transient blackboard presentation streams the current draft to the other participants, who render the same editor read-only; Save persists one incremental CRDT change and clears the transient draft. SCRIPTA document updates are invalidation signals rather than serialized viewer projections: each receiver reloads the authoritative blackboard with its own authenticated identity before rendering viewer votes or owner-only edit/delete controls.

`webmeet_blackboard_get` is the projection read path and `webmeet_event_command` is the sole mutation path. The authenticated-only read tool `webmeet_scripta_workspace_list` supplies folders and Markdown paths to the picker and rejects guests. Runtime and dependency trees such as `.data`, `.git`, `.ploinky`, and `node_modules` are excluded. Folder and document result limits are independent, so a large directory tree cannot consume the document allowance before Markdown files are discovered. The Other picker displays only `.md` files returned from the workspace, supports filtering by file name or folder, and permits selection only from that list. UI actions, canonical `/event` JSON, and multilingual `/robo` text all target the singleton widget and use the same versioned event executor. A room has exactly one attached SCRIPTA document: creating or opening another document releases the previous one automatically. Document create, open, and physical delete; view navigation; variant operations; votes; and structural chapter/paragraph operations never write through a UI-specific path. Physical deletion requires explicit confirmation.

The deterministic `/event <allowlisted-action> [JSON object]` form, including the form rendered for event entries in Full chat, is parsed directly without AI. Other natural text is interpreted by one isolated Ploinky `MainAgent` executing the local `blackboard-event` code skill. The skill receives a path-free SCRIPTA context and returns one canonical event or a clarification. It may use only identifiers in context and paths explicitly supplied by an authorized user. A missing create path selects the room folder. AI generation or reformulation failure leaves both the room attachment state and workspace unchanged.

Every admitted participant may request RoboTeam operations. Guests work only with room-visible resource identifiers and titles. They may create in the room folder, ask RoboTeam to open a room-folder document by title, and operate on attached documents, but they never receive the workspace tree or a document path. RoboTeam resolves a guest title against the direct Markdown children of the room folder server-side. Every SCRIPTA action binds the caller-supplied LiveKit participant identity to the authenticated room member before it creates a viewer projection, checks variant ownership, or records a vote. The stable owner hash remains internal; public projections contain only viewer-relative `canEdit` and `canDelete` flags. Blackboard, resource, and chat event projections omit workspace paths, Explorer identifiers, owner hashes, and editor URLs for guests.

Each command carries `eventId`, `commandId`, `expectedBoardVersion`, a widget target, an allowlisted action, and a bounded payload. AI interpretation occurs outside the room lock. Version checks and mutations occur inside the room lock, repeated command IDs are idempotent, and accepted mutations regenerate the singleton widget and emit `blackboard.updated` immediately. The authoritative widget projection is rendered before cached browser replicas synchronize. Inline edits use a mutation queue keyed by resource, wait for any pending pull, and update their next Automerge heads only after the preceding edit is acknowledged. View-only navigation does not pull an unchanged replica. Explorer updates an existing public collaboration document through granular Automerge changes and never rebuilds or reassigns the complete model merely because canonical metadata differs, preserving one continuous and compact history. Successful incremental pull and apply responses contain changes and heads only; snapshots are included only with `resetRequired`, and widget projection comes from the mutation response. Pull sends incremental changes only when every client head belongs to that history. An unknown head returns `resetRequired`; the browser then replaces its path-free local replica with the authoritative snapshot. The browser also verifies that applying incremental changes produces the advertised server heads and explicitly reopens a snapshot only if local application fails. Canonical undo records the pre-operation heads, post-operation heads, and a digest of the resulting editable model. It restores the historical view as a new change only while that digest still matches; if Advanced Editor or another participant changed the document afterward, undo returns a conflict instead of overwriting the newer work. Complete document snapshots are never embedded in the canonical document. The panel applies the blackboard already returned by a successful event rather than issuing a redundant blackboard read. The SCRIPTA UI permits only one in-flight structural action per panel; inline text is projected locally on save and reconciled with the authoritative response. A version conflict updates the adapter version, reloads the authoritative board, and surfaces the structured server message without an unhandled promise rejection. Normal chat hides event traffic, while Full interleaves the safe semantic event commands and participant discussion in timestamp order.

Explorer is the sole authority for SCRIPTA workspace files and Automerge state. WebMeet never imports filesystem APIs for SCRIPTA and never parses or serializes the Markdown file. It calls Explorer through the router-mediated `AgentMcpClient` and an internal-only allowlist containing `scripta_crdt_ensure_folder`, `scripta_crdt_workspace_list`, `scripta_crdt_create`, `scripta_crdt_open`, `scripta_crdt_mutate`, and `scripta_crdt_delete`. Explorer creates the Markdown, canonical Automerge state, and path-free collaboration replica as one failure-safe operation; if replica creation fails, the newly created artifacts are removed. If Explorer creation succeeds but the subsequent room save fails, WebMeet preserves the valid workspace document and returns `scripta_attachment_failed` with `documentCreated: true` and `attached: false`, so the user can open the existing document instead of creating a duplicate. The mutation tool applies the semantic change and saves the Markdown in one Explorer-serialized CRDT transaction. Physical deletion uses a prepare/commit transaction: Explorer stages Markdown, canonical Automerge state, and the collaboration replica, WebMeet removes the room attachment, and Explorer commits only after the room save succeeds; either failure restores the staged document and room projection. An expired pending deletion is restored conservatively during store recovery. Explorer serializes all lifecycle operations for one document with a token-owned lock based only on its canonical workspace path and removes stale locks only when the recorded owner is no longer alive. WebMeet uses a cross-process attachment lock keyed by the requested document path only around create/open/delete availability changes, so different documents and rooms remain independent. Create and open reuse Explorer's returned projection instead of opening the same CRDT document a second time. The room stores only attachment metadata, persistent focus, audit data, and the safe widget projection.

The reusable SCRIPTA state and document semantics live under `explorer/shared/document`; Advanced Editor, Explorer's CRDT service, and the shared variants UI use that allowlisted source. WebMeet has no copied state module and no fallback parser. Generic filesystem tools are not part of the RoboTeam path, and there is no legacy `roomId + documentRole` compatibility contract.

In paragraph mode, the document header centers one auxiliary position indicator in the form `Chapter N - Paragraph N`. The paragraph context area keeps only the actual chapter title; separate chapter and paragraph ordinal labels are not rendered beside editable content.

The contextual chapter action group presents paragraph creation as `Paragraph +`. Chapter up/down use explicit reorder-list icons without an additional visible label; their titles and accessible labels identify the operations as `Move chapter up` and `Move chapter down`.

Paragraph up/down uses the same reorder-list icon language as chapter movement, while its titles and accessible labels remain `Move paragraph up` and `Move paragraph down`.

Creating a chapter or paragraph changes the persistent document focus to the newly generated stable ID and returns to document view. The projection carries the target type and creation revision; each client scrolls the new element into the nearest visible area and focuses its chapter title or paragraph control once for that revision.

## Decisions & Questions

### Question #1: Why are Vision and Plan templates instead of document types?

Response:
They differ only in generated initial structure. Keeping one document format prevents editing, voting, navigation, and rendering rules from branching by creation history.

### Question #2: Why must UI operations pass through RoboTeam?

Response:
One event path gives chat and direct controls identical authorization, optimistic concurrency, audit, realtime delivery, and persistence behavior.

## Conclusion

SCRIPTA collaboration remains correct while workspace Markdown is authoritative, the blackboard uses one document widget, all mutations use canonical RoboTeam events, creation templates do not become document roles, and guest projections remain path-free.
